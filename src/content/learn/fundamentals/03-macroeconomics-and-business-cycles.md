## 1. Macroeconomic Drivers: Growth, Inflation & Rates

Macroeconomic forces dictate the cost of capital, systemic liquidity, and market valuation multiples.

```
          [THE MONETARY & ECONOMIC CYCLE]
  Economic Expansion -> Inflation Accelerates -> Central Bank Hikes Rates
         ^                                                   |
         |                                                   v
  Central Bank Cuts Rates <- Recession / Slowdown <- Liquidity Contracts
```

### Core Macro Variables
1. **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**: Measures aggregate economic output. Real GDP isolates volume growth by removing inflation.
2. **Inflation Metrics (CPI vs. PPI / WPI)**:
   - **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**: Measures headline inflation on household baskets.
   - **Core CPI**: Strips out volatile food and energy to reveal structural wage and service inflation.
3. **Monetary Policy Stance**:
   - **Quantitative Easing (QE)**: Central bank expands balance sheet by purchasing sovereign debt; floods banking system with reserves, lowering long-term bond yields.
   - **Quantitative Tightening (QT)**: Central bank lets bonds mature without reinvestment or sells assets; drains systemic liquidity.

## 2. Yield Curve Mechanics & Recession Signaling

The yield curve plots sovereign bond interest rates across maturities ranging from 3 months to 30 years.

```
Yield (%)
  |                  /------------ [Normal Upward-Sloping Curve]
  |                 /               (Healthy economic expansion)
  |                /
  |  =============/=============== [Flat Curve - Transition State]
  |
  |  \
  |   \
  |    \-------------------------- [Inverted Curve]
  |                                (10Y yield < 2Y yield; Recession warning)
--+----------------------------------------> Maturity (2Y, 5Y, 10Y, 30Y)
```

### The Inverted Yield Curve (10Y minus 2Y)
- **Normal Curve**: Investors demand higher yields for locking up money in longer maturities (due to inflation risk and duration risk).
- **Inverted Curve**: Occurs when short-term yields spike above long-term yields. This happens when the central bank hikes policy rates aggressively to combat inflation, while bond markets price in eventual economic contraction and future rate cuts.
- **Predictive Power**: An inversion in the US 10Y-2Y yield spread (or 10Y-3M spread) has preceded every recession for the past 60 years with an average lead time of 12 to 18 months. The recession typically begins not when the curve inverts, but when it abruptly **un-inverts** (steepens) as emergency rate cuts commence.

## 3. Sector Rotation Across Business Cycle Stages

Capital flows systematically across different market sectors depending on the current phase of the economic cycle.

```
       [SECTOR ROTATION ACROSS THE BUSINESS CYCLE]

+--------------------+---------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
| 1. EARLY CYCLE     | 2. MID CYCLE        | 3. LATE CYCLE      | 4. RECESSION       |
| (Recovery)         | (Peak Growth)       | (Overheating)      | (Contraction)      |
+--------------------+---------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
| Interest Rates Low | Rates Neutral/Rising| Rates High/Tight   | Rates Cutting      |
| Credit Expanding   | Strongest Earnings  | Inflation Peaking  | Earnings Plummet   |
+--------------------+---------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
| WINNING SECTORS:   | WINNING SECTORS:    | WINNING SECTORS:   | WINNING SECTORS:   |
| * Financials/Banks | * Technology        | * Energy           | * Healthcare       |
| * Consumer Discret.| * Industrials       | * Materials        | * Utilities        |
| * Real Estate      | * Capital Goods     | * Commodities      | * Consumer Staples |
+--------------------+---------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
```

### Strategic Sector Characteristics
- **Cyclical Growth (Early/Mid)**:
  - *Consumer Discretionary / Auto*: Consumers finance durable goods as credit loosens.
  - *Financials*: Banks benefit from credit expansion and expanding net interest margins.
- **Inflation & Resource Plays (Late)**:
  - *Energy & Materials*: Late-cycle supply bottlenecks drive commodity prices to multi-year highs.
- **Defensive Capital Preservation (Recession)**:
  - *Utilities, Healthcare, Consumer Staples*: Inelastic demand profiles. Consumers continue buying medicine, electricity, and basic groceries regardless of unemployment levels. Cash flows remain insulated.

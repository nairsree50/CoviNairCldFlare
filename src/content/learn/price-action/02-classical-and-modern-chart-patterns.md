## 1. Classical Reversal Formations

Reversal patterns indicate that the underlying balance between supply and demand has shifted, signaling an impending change in the primary trend.

### Head and Shoulders (H&S)

```
                            HEAD (H)
                               /\
            LEFT SHOULDER     /  \     RIGHT SHOULDER
                 (LS)        /    \         (RS)
                  /\        /      \         /\
                 /  \      /        \       /  \
                /    \    /          \     /    \
Neckline ======X======\==/============\===X======\====== [Breakdown Point]
                       \/              \/         \
                                                   v [Measured Move Target]
```

- **Anatomy Rules**:
  1. **Left Shoulder**: Strong rally on expanding volume followed by a minor secondary reaction pullback.
  2. **Head**: Price rallies to print a higher high, but often on slightly less volume than the Left Shoulder.
  3. **Right Shoulder**: Rally fails to reach the Head and stalls roughly near the height of the Left Shoulder on light volume.
  4. **Neckline**: Drawn connecting the two reaction reaction swing lows. Can be horizontal, upward-sloping, or downward-sloping.
- **Execution Rules**:
  - *Trigger*: Decisive closing bar below the neckline (or wait for a low-volume retest of the broken neckline from beneath).
  - *Stop Loss*: Placed above the peak of the Right Shoulder.
  - *Measured Move Target*: Measure the vertical distance from the top of the Head to the Neckline; project that exact distance downward from the breakout point.

### Double Top & Double Bottom

```
          DOUBLE TOP                              DOUBLE BOTTOM
        Peak 1      Peak 2
          /\          /\                       Neckline ===================
         /  \        /  \                                \        /
        /    \  /\  /    \                                \  /\  /
Neckline =====\/==\/======\====                            \/  \/
                           v [Target]                   Trough 1  Trough 2
```

- **Validation Rule**: The two peaks must have distinct separation in time (at least 3-4 weeks on a daily chart). Peak 2 should print with noticeably lower volume and an oscillator divergence (RSI or MACD).
- **Target**: Vertical height of the formation projected downward from the broken neckline.

## 2. Classical Continuation Formations

Continuation patterns represent temporary pauses or breathers within an established trending regime.

```
       BULL FLAG                               ASCENDING TRIANGLE
         /\   \                                         ------------------- (Flat Resistance)
        /  \   \                                       /        /        /
       /    \   \  <-- Parallel Flag                  /   /\   /   /\   /
      /      \   \     Consolidation                 /   /  \ /   /  \ /
     /        \   \                                 /---/----v---/----v----- (Rising Support)
[Flagpole]     \   \
```

### Bull & Bear Flags
- **Flagpole**: A sharp, high-volume near-vertical impulse leg.
- **Flag Body**: A tight, orderly consolidation channel sloping counter to the preceding trend on contracting volume.
- **Trigger**: Buy when price breaks out of the upper channel line of the flag on expanding volume.
- **Target**: The full length of the preceding flagpole added to the breakout point.

### Triangles (Ascending, Descending, Symmetrical)
- **Ascending Triangle**: Horizontal upper resistance ceiling combined with an ascending line of higher lows. Bullish accumulation; buy the breakout above horizontal resistance.
- **Descending Triangle**: Flat support floor combined with descending lower highs. Bearish distribution; short the breakdown below horizontal support.
- **Symmetrical Triangle**: Two converging trendlines (lower highs and higher lows). Neutral coil; wait for a confirmed directional breakout before entering.

## 3. Modern Institutional Price Action (ICT / SMC)

Smart-money concepts examine where institutional algorithmic orders exist in the order book.

### Fair Value Gap (FVG) / Price Imbalance

An FVG occurs when aggressive institutional market orders cause an explosive price move, leaving a gap where only one side of the market was filled.

```
       [BULLISH FAIR VALUE GAP - 3-CANDLE SEQUENCE]

           Candle 3 (High of Wick) ------+
                                         |
               [FAIR VALUE GAP]          | <-- Unmitigated Imbalance Zone
               (Zero Overlap)            |     (Future Institutional Support)
                                         |
           Candle 1 (Low of Wick) -------+
```

- **Identification**:
  1. **Candle 1**: Preceding candle.
  2. **Candle 2**: Large, impulsive displacement candle.
  3. **Candle 3**: Subsequent candle.
  4. **The Gap**: The space between the high of Candle 1 and the low of Candle 3. There is zero price overlap between them.
- **Execution Rule**: When price subsequently pulls back into the FVG (called "mitigation"), look for lower-timeframe reversal candles to join the trend. Place stop below Candle 2's origin.

### Liquidity Sweeps / Stop Hunts

```
Retail Stop Losses / Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL)
-------------------------------------x--x--- (Prior Swing Highs)
                                    / \
                                   /   \ <-- Swift Rejection Back Inside Range
                                  /     \
Prior Trading Range =============/=======\=====================================
```

- **Mechanics**: Large institutional market participants require deep liquidity to fill massive size. They push price momentarily through obvious support/resistance levels where retail stop-loss orders pool.
- **The Footprint**: Price pushes beyond a prior swing high or low, triggers all pending stop orders, and immediately reverses, closing back inside the range with a long wick.
- **Execution Rule**: When a sweep of a prior major swing high occurs and the candle closes back below the level with a bearish rejection, enter short targeting the opposing liquidity pool (prior swing low).

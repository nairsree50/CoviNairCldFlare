## The "Starting at ₹85 Lakh" Illusion

Picture driving along an arterial expressway in Bengaluru, Pune, or Gurugram. 

Every hundred meters, towering hoardings flash seductive real estate advertisements: *"Luxurious 2BHK Homes Starting at ₹85 Lakh! Book Today with Just ₹1 Lakh Down!"*

For a first-time homebuyer who has spent five disciplined years accumulating an ₹18 lakh down payment, that billboard looks like an open door to domestic stability. You do the mental arithmetic: an ₹85 lakh flat with a 20% down payment (₹17 lakh) leaves an easily manageable ₹68 lakh bank loan.

You schedule a site visit, walk through a beautifully furnished show apartment with mood lighting and Italian marble, and sit down at the sales gallery desk. 

The sales executive smiles and slides an official "All-Inclusive Cost Sheet" across the glass table.

As you scan down the column of figures, a cold knot tightens in your stomach. 

There are lines for Floor Rise Charges, Preferred Location Charges, Covered Car Parking, Clubhouse Development Fees, Advance Maintenance, Electricity Substation Infrastructure, Legal Documentation, Stamp Duty, and Goods and Services Tax (GST).

The final figure printed at the bottom of the page is not ₹85,00,000. 

It reads **₹1,07,45,000**.

Before you have bought a single curtain or installed a modular kitchen cabinet, your dream flat has expanded by over **₹22 lakh in hidden add-ons**. 

If your savings account only holds your original ₹18 lakh down payment, you suddenly face an immediate cash shortfall that can derail the entire purchase.

## Bucket 1: Statutory Government Levies (7% to 12%)

These are mandatory state and central government fees that cannot be negotiated away, discounted, or waived.

### 1. Stamp Duty (5% to 7%)
Stamp duty is a state-level transaction tax payable upon registering the property conveyance deed. 

Rates vary significantly across Indian states:
* Maharashtra: ~6% to 7% (including metro cess and local body taxes)
* Karnataka: ~5.6%
* Delhi: ~4% to 6% (with modest concessions for female registrants)

On an ₹85 lakh base property in a major metro, stamp duty alone demands roughly **₹4.5 lakh to ₹5.5 lakh** in upfront cash.

### 2. Registration Charges (1%)
Paid to the sub-registrar office to legally record the transaction in state land records. While some states cap registration charges, in cities like Bengaluru, it adds a flat **1% of property value**—another **₹85,000**.

### 3. Goods and Services Tax (GST)
If you purchase an **under-construction property**, the central government levies GST:
* **Standard Residential Housing:** **5%** (without input tax credit)
* **Affordable Housing (< ₹45 Lakh and < 60-90 sq.m):** **1%**

On an under-construction flat priced at ₹85 lakh, GST adds an immediate **₹4,25,000** to your cost sheet.

*Key Exception:* If you purchase a completed, ready-to-move property that has already received its official **Occupancy Certificate (OC)** from the local development authority, **zero GST is payable**. Buying a ready home with an OC instantly eliminates this 5% penalty.

## Bucket 2: The Builder's "Hidden Menu" (6% to 10%)

Real estate developers quote low Base Selling Prices on advertising hoardings to draw foot traffic, then recover their profit margins through non-negotiable amenities and location charges.

### 1. Covered Car Parking Allocation (₹3,00,000 to ₹6,00,000)
While Supreme Court rulings have held that open stilt parking cannot be sold as independent commercial units, developers routinely charge a mandatory "parking space allocation fee." In high-density metro societies, a single covered basement parking slot costs anywhere from ₹3 lakh to ₹6 lakh.

### 2. Preferred Location Charges (PLC)
If the apartment faces an internal landscaped garden, a clubhouse swimming pool, or an open park, the builder charges an additional premium ranging from **₹150 to ₹400 per square foot**. For a 1,200 sq.ft 2BHK, a garden view adds ₹2.5 lakh to ₹4.5 lakh.

### 3. Floor Rise Charges (FRC)
Developers charge a premium for higher floors to capitalize on open views and reduced street noise. A common structure is **₹25 to ₹50 per square foot per floor** starting from the fourth floor. Choosing an apartment on the 16th floor can add ₹3,50,000 to your purchase price.

### 4. Clubhouse and Amenities Usage Fees (₹2,00,000 to ₹4,00,000)
Access to the gymnasium, swimming pool, and banquet hall is billed as a one-time non-refundable capital development charge, typically running between ₹2 lakh and ₹4 lakh.

### 5. Infrastructure and Utility Connection Fees (₹1,50,000 to ₹3,00,000)
Charges for installing dedicated electricity substations, back-up diesel generators, municipal water connections, and piped gas meters are passed straight along to buyers.

### 6. Advance Maintenance and Sinking Fund (IFMS)
Most developers require buyers to deposit twelve to twenty-four months of maintenance fees upfront into an **Interest-Free Maintenance Security (IFMS)** account. At ₹3 to ₹5 per square foot monthly, a 1,200 sq.ft flat demands an advance lump sum of **₹75,000 to ₹1,40,000** before you receive possession keys.

## Bucket 3: Lending and Legal Fees (₹40,000 to ₹1,00,000)

Securing a mortgage and verifying legal ownership carries its own administrative toll:

* **Bank Processing and Administrative Fees:** Banks charge between **0.25% and 0.50%** of the sanctioned loan amount, plus 18% GST. On a ₹70 lakh loan, processing fees range from ₹17,500 to ₹35,000.
* **Valuation and Title Search Fees:** The lender dispatches an external civil engineer to inspect the site and a legal advocate to review parent property deeds, billing you ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 for the privilege.
* **Independent Legal Due Diligence:** Never rely exclusively on the bank's legal approval. A bank's lawyer verifies only that the bank's loan is recoverable; they do not audit building plan deviations that could invite municipal demolition notices. Hiring an independent real estate lawyer to scrutinize land titles, encumbrance certificates (ECs), and RERA approvals costs ₹15,000 to ₹30,000—and is worth every single rupee.

## Bucket 4: The Fit-Out Reality (₹8,00,000 to ₹15,00,000)

When a developer hands you the keys to a newly constructed apartment, you do not receive a cozy home. 

You receive a cold concrete shell. 

There are bare plaster walls, exposed electrical wiring conduits, an empty kitchen counter with no cabinets, and bare bedrooms lacking storage.

Transforming that bare shell into a functional living space requires substantial capital:
* **Modular Kitchen (Cabinets, chimney, hobs, granite counter finishes):** ₹2,50,000 to ₹4,50,000
* **Master and Guest Wardrobes (Woodwork and storage):** ₹3,00,000 to ₹5,50,000
* **Electrical Fixtures (Air conditioners, ceiling fans, lighting, geysers):** ₹1,80,000 to ₹3,00,000
* **Safety Grills and Mosquito Mesh:** ₹40,000 to ₹80,000
* **Curtains, Painting Touch-Ups, and Deep Cleaning:** ₹60,000 to ₹1,20,000

A modest, high-utility interior execution for a 2BHK flat easily commands **₹8,00,000 to ₹12,00,000**. 

If you fail to budget for interiors upfront, you are forced to fund carpentry using high-interest personal loans or credit card EMI schemes at 16% to 24% interest.

## The First-Time Homebuyer Rule of Thumb

To ensure you never fall into an affordability trap, memorize this basic budgeting rule:

**Always add 25% to 30% to the builder's advertised base price to calculate your true capital requirement.**

If an apartment is marketed at ₹1 Crore, assume your all-inclusive outgo will reach ₹1.25 to ₹1.30 Crore.

Equally important: **Banks only finance 75% to 80% of the agreement value.** 

They do not fund stamp duty, registration charges, advance maintenance deposits, or custom interior carpentry. All of those secondary expenses must come straight out of your personal liquid savings.

Walk into property negotiations with your eyes wide open. Demand a detailed, line-item cost sheet before handing over a single rupee of token money, verify every statutory fee, and protect your family's financial freedom by budgeting for the complete journey from bare concrete to housewarming.
